# Unison’s Approach to ESG and Our Framework

Since its inception, Unison has pursued impact and social value that go beyond economic profits as a core business objective. This is also clearly stated in our *Guiding Principles*.

> While we rigorously pursue profitable opportunities, we aim to make an impact on the business community we are involved in.
>
> We seek to create business transactions which have value not only to ourselves but also to society.

Based on this philosophy, we have consistently implemented initiatives since Fund I under the belief that measures such as improving working environments and boosting employee morale at our portfolio companies lead to stronger long-term performance and enhanced corporate value. As ESG initiatives have gained importance within the private equity industry, we established our ESG policy in 2016, and since 2017 we have made disclosures as a PRI signatory. In 2020, we established Unison’s proprietary ESG framework and began rolling it out across our portfolio. At present, we are working to make our impact more visible, and over time we aim to quantitatively demonstrate that our ESG initiatives have a positive effect on investment returns.

We believe that private equity investing provides an optimal governance model for advancing ESG initiatives. As a control investor, we are in a position to implement the ESG agenda we pursue within our portfolio companies, and we recognize that we bear a greater responsibility than investors in listed equities. In particular, among the mid-market and SME businesses that Unison targets, many companies lag behind relative to their business scale in terms of organizational and facility infrastructure; enhancing these foundations directly contributes to improved performance and corporate value. We also believe that pursuing the kind of corporate profile that is highly valued by public market investors—or that public companies value when deciding to bring a company into their group as a subsidiary—not only from business and financial perspectives but also in relation to society and the environment, leads to better outcomes at exit. While ESG initiatives among Japanese private equity funds remain a work in progress, Unison has participated in the launch of the ESG Committee of the Japan Private Equity Association, and we proactively plan study sessions and facilitate the sharing of best practices among GPs to move the industry’s efforts forward.

## Two-Stage “CDE” Framework and Monitoring Approach

Unison has established the following two-stage framework for ESG initiatives, premised on control investments in mid-sized and small companies, and has rolled it out across our portfolio companies.

### [Stage 1] Community, Diversity & Empowerment

- C: Contribution to the local communities and customer communities in which the company operates

- D: Ensuring workforce diversity. Increasing the ratio of women in management positions and above. Developing a board skill matrix and proactively appointing outside directors from outside Unison

- E: Improving the working environment, and ensuring fair evaluation and compensation. Empowering employees through information sharing, etc.

### [Stage 2] Carbon, Disposables & Energy

- C: Reduction of carbon emissions through corporate activities

- D: Reduction in the use of plastics and other environmentally harmful materials

- E: Improving the efficiency of energy consumption, including water; proactive adoption of renewable energy, etc.

For Unison’s portfolio companies, which are not necessarily large in scale, delivering visible improvements in environmental impact from the outset can be organizationally challenging. We therefore begin by working together to design and implement Stage 1 initiatives—where companies can drive clear improvements through their own efforts—thereby lowering the barrier to ESG engagement and connecting these efforts to initiatives that management teams can more readily feel the impact of, such as talent acquisition and employee retention. Once the organization has experienced a certain level of effectiveness, we move forward with Stage 2 initiatives centered on environmental impact.

For both Stage 1 and Stage 2, specific initiatives are set for each portfolio company in line with its mission statement and investment themes, and tailored to its scale and organizational circumstances. In designing these initiatives, we reference materiality and best practices in each industry, and set goals and milestones for each portfolio company. By incorporating this process into business plan development immediately after investment as well as subsequent business plan reviews during the investment period, and adding it to management’s annual qualitative evaluation, we conduct continuous monitoring in the same way as we do for business KPIs.

## Visualization of Impact through Quantification

Unison quantifies and accumulates data on ESG initiatives through the following methods.

### RIMM Sustainability

To visualize the ESG initiatives of our portfolio companies, Unison co-developed a platform for GPs with RIMM Sustainability (“RIMM”).

RIMM’s mission is to “make sustainability simple, accessible, fun, and rewarding—for companies of all sizes.” Specifically, it provides a software platform service that visualizes, quantifies, and evaluates corporate sustainability initiatives in accordance with international standards such as the Global Reporting Initiative (GRI) and the Sustainability Accounting Standards Board (SASB).

RIMM’s platform combines information on sustainability initiatives provided by the companies being assessed with sustainability data on global public and private companies contained in RIMM’s database, using a proprietary algorithm. It then visualizes the assessed company’s initiatives in the form of “RIMM Scores” and “SDGs Scores.” The feedback reports also present the current state and areas for improvement together with case studies from industry peers, designed to translate insights into concrete actions.

The GP dashboard allows users to view each portfolio company’s responses and benchmarking results side-by-side in a single overview. By drilling down into the drivers behind lagging KPIs and encouraging the adoption of best practices from other portfolio companies, it helps advance initiatives across the portfolio.

In addition to Unison’s proprietary two-stage CDE framework, conducting third-party assessments through RIMM and benchmarking against industry peers has helped ensure that each portfolio company’s ESG initiatives do not become self-contained, but instead evolve with a clear focus on the material issues prioritized within their respective industries. RIMM’s platform also supports the automatic creation of sustainability reports based on data accumulated over time, enabling use by portfolio companies that plan to pursue an IPO or external disclosures in the future.

### ESG Data Convergence Initiative

Unison is the first Japanese GP to participate in the ESG Data Convergence Initiative (the “EDCI”), under which we provide data annually.

The EDCI is a framework in which approximately 450 GPs and LPs, including major global PE firms and asset managers, collaborate to collect and report key ESG metrics in a standardized manner. It is the first PE industry-led initiative to standardize GP disclosure metrics and create a database that enables performance comparisons.

Ahead of joining the EDCI, Unison began measuring GHG emissions across all portfolio companies. While some companies with in-house production facilities had already been measuring Scope 1 (direct) and Scope 2 (indirect) emissions, this was a first-time effort for most of our portfolio companies. Where in-house conversion was not feasible, we introduced external emissions measurement services to collect and digitize data on direct and indirect emissions. We benchmarked the measurement results through both RIMM and the EDCI; however, concrete initiatives to reduce emissions are still to come, and in the future we will also need to expand our efforts to identify Scope 3 emissions, including those across the supply chain.

Based on the data collected, the EDCI conducts benchmarking against public companies. While PE-backed companies appear to lag at the starting point, it has been confirmed that their degree of improvement and magnitude of change during the investment period exceed those of public companies. By quantitatively demonstrating the ability of PE funds making control investments to drive organizational change, and by enabling each GP’s track record to be reviewed under a consistent set of definitions, the EDCI helps ensure transparency in ESG disclosures—where greenwashing is an ever-present concern.

Sustainability initiatives are not temporary. They are activities that each company should continuously advance even after Unison’s investment reaches its exit. Using quantification and benchmarking as the foundation, we repeatedly engage in discussions with portfolio company management to ensure that each company’s initiatives remain sustainable, and we carry these efforts forward every day.

# ESG Initiatives in the Consumer Sector

Since the days of Fund I, Unison has invested in a wide range of consumer goods companies and multi-store business models. The themes we have pursued in supporting these portfolio companies and enhancing their value have included the origins of Unison’s ESG approach.

For example, at Tohato, a long-established confectionery manufacturer in Fund I for which we supported Japan’s first pre-packaged civil rehabilitation, we created a brand book for employees titled *Okashi wo Shigoto ni Dekiru Koufuku (The Joy of Making Confectionery Your Work)* as part of the company's turnaround. It was a pop-up book created to communicate "the joy of turning what you love into your work" and "the meaning of working as a team," and to foster a shared sense of purpose. The catchphrase "Rather than becoming an organization, let's become a team," coined by Hidetoshi Nakata upon assuming the role of Chief Branding Officer, supported the organization as a spiritual pillar of management reform.

Akindo Sushiro, a Fund II portfolio company that achieved the No.1 customer satisfaction ranking in the restaurant industry, grew during our investment period from the third-largest to the largest conveyor-belt sushi chain in Japan. This rapid growth was underpinned by an “inverted pyramid organization with stores at the top.” Rather than issuing top-down directives, management clearly articulated a stance that headquarters’ role is to thoroughly support frontline staff who interact with customers every day, and improved a range of company systems, including HR programs and labor management.

Because the consumer sector spans a broad range of industries and each subsector has its own depth, a “one size fits all” approach does not work. While Unison currently measures and quantifies common ESG metrics across all portfolio companies, in the consumer sector in particular we focus on building ESG approaches tailored to each portfolio company’s business model and needs. In the following pages, we introduce initiatives at Pyuru and Daytona International as concrete examples of Unison’s approach in close alignment with our portfolio companies.

## Pyuru: Pursuing Effective Clean Beauty

Pyuru, in which Unison invested in June 2019, develops, manufactures, and sells hair care products and skincare cosmetics made with natural ingredients in an integrated manner. Since its founding in 1998, the company has focused on developing and producing natural-brand products that are gentle on hair and skin while delivering strong coloring performance, as exemplified by the Rishiri Hair Color Series, the best-selling gray-hair color-treatment brand in Japan for 13 consecutive years. The new management team, which succeeded the founder after the transfer of shares to Unison and his retirement, has formulated a more modern mission and vision while preserving the company's founding spirit.

Since its founding, Pyuru has developed products using natural ingredients, and it has articulated that concept through its proprietary philosophy of Effective Clean Beauty. The mission and vision formulated on that basis clearly express Pyuru's commitment both to supporting beauty beyond age and to providing globally competitive products that are highly effective and environmentally friendly. They define the core of Pyuru's brand development, research and development, and ESG initiatives, and have become a driving force for its next stage of growth. On the importance of renewing the organization for its next stage while preserving the founder's values and establishing a common language across the company, President Inoue commented as follows:

> "We have long stood alongside one of the most common hair concerns: gray hair. By steadily pursuing the initiatives set out in our mission and vision, we have grown into a brand that continues to be chosen by many people, resulting in 13 consecutive years as the No. 1 brand in Japan by sales. In recent years, we have also launched new brands that address younger consumers' concerns, including gray hair, post-COVID hair loss, postpartum hair loss, and scalp concerns among younger people who enjoy fashion. Going forward, we aim to develop products that help address the concerns of many people, including issues that can become personal insecurities as well as broader social challenges. Based on our belief that, for products to continue being chosen over the long term, they must be not only kind to people and the environment but also genuinely effective, we will continue to pursue the realization of Effective Clean Beauty."

### Focus on Raw Materials and Environmental Initiatives in the Manufacturing and Development Process

With its mission and vision clearly defined, Pyuru began taking steps to embody the philosophy behind them. Its head office factory, newly established in 2017, is located in the naturally rich area of Itoshima, Fukuoka Prefecture, enabling the company to control and manage much of its manufacturing process and energy consumption in-house. Pyuru already uses renewable energy such as solar and wind power, which covers around 10% of the electricity it requires. In addition, it has introduced energy-saving measures such as controlling the number of air-conditioning units and air compressors in operation according to usage, and it plans to further increase the use of renewable energy. Through these efforts, Pyuru aims to achieve net-zero greenhouse gas emissions by 2026.

As part of its efforts to reduce environmental impact, Pyuru has achieved zero emissions in waste disposal by sorting and recycling raw-material containers and cardboard generated in the production process, as well as office waste. In addition, at its manufacturing site, Pyuru has established a production system capable of meeting the standards of ECOCERT COSMOS certification\*, a globally recognized benchmark for organic products. It is also continuously improving its operations based on feedback and suggestions from each individual involved in production, thereby updating its manufacturing processes in line with global environmental standards.

Pyuru has also established its own standards for ingredients and packaging used in its products. For example, it does not use microplastics, phthalate esters, parabens, mineral oils, or sulfate-based surfactants. In addition, RISHIRIA Furel, a new product targeting younger consumers and a sister brand to the Rishiri series, succeeded in reducing plastic usage by approximately 70% through innovations in its refill container design. Pyuru has also partnered with local farms to launch its own farm, where it cultivates herbs and plants used in its products. Employees participate in work at the farm, deepening engagement with the local community.

When developing new products, Pyuru establishes guiding principles aligned with Effective Clean Beauty.

- Use ingredients that are considerate of both people and the environment
- Place importance on scientific evidence
- Continue listening to and engaging with customer feedback
- Thoroughly localize across countries, environments, and cultures

LIHAW, one of the new brands launched in 2021, uses fragrances extracted from plants and herbs grown on Pyuru’s own farm, offering high-quality, effective skincare products at affordable prices. Wicot, its professional-use line, is Japan’s first serum to receive ECOCERT COSMOS certification, and it is also developed and manufactured in-house.

### Regional Job Creation and Fair Evaluation System

Pyuru’s head office and factory employ a significant number of women, contributing to job creation in the Itoshima region. As part of a review of its performance evaluation system, more part-time employees have been promoted to full-time positions, and the proportion of women in management has steadily increased since Unison’s investment. Pyuru also encourages men to take childcare leave, achieving a 100% childcare leave usage rate among male employees since 2020. The company is also advancing initiatives to share internally the joy of being involved in hair care and skincare products, such as holding makeup classes for employees.

“An employee-friendly working environment is created by implementing initiatives that are not bound by organizational customs or stereotypes—such as evaluating employees fairly regardless of gender, age, or length of service, and promoting efforts that enhance work-life balance. Promoting women into management roles and encouraging men to take childcare leave are examples of this,” says CFO Tominaga. Specific initiatives include introducing four types of surveys—such as a “360-degree survey,” where employees write what they would like colleagues to “continue doing” and what they would like them to “change,” and a survey for “requests from subordinates to supervisors”—as well as conducting HR interviews twice a year. Management reviews all feedback and provides timely responses to respondents, along with improvement actions. As an HR policy, Pyuru will continue to pursue an environment where employees with a strong willingness to demonstrate competencies and a commitment to results can thrive, by removing noise from evaluations such as past experience, track record, and gender.

### In Conclusion

The formulation of Pyuru’s mission, vision, and subsequent ESG policies clarified the value the company provides through its business activities and how it connects with the world. This has built a solid foundation for Pyuru to advance to its next growth stage while remaining true to its founding principles. Pyuru’s ESG initiatives are closely linked to making it a company of choice for both consumers and employees, and we expect Pyuru to further develop its unique initiatives even after Unison’s investment period ends.

## Daytona International: Engaging Communities with Field-Led Initiatives

Daytona International, in which Unison invested in April 2021, operates a boutique-style retail apparel business. With its core business, FREAK'S STORE, Daytona has established a unique brand world and positioning, and continues to expand in the lifestyle domain with fashion at its center. While Daytona’s success—such as e-commerce accounting for more than 60% of sales—is often discussed in the context of DX/OMO, the company has also set the goal of “a prosperous future shared among all stakeholders” to earn support from environmentally conscious generations in the apparel industry, which is generally considered to have a high environmental impact. Daytona is focusing on the following four areas:

- Community: Maximizing and communicating the attractiveness of local regions
- Value Chain: Collective responsible action
- People: Enabling diverse individuality to shine
- Environment: Passing on a thriving planet to the next generation

In particular, in its environmental initiatives, Daytona has set the goal of becoming "a company that leaves little waste behind," aiming to eliminate waste across the entire value chain and create a new circular economy. Daytona is tackling head-on the issue of product disposal, an industry practice that poses major challenges from both an environmental and a business standpoint. By improving demand forecasting and selling through products in a nimble manner based on customer needs, Daytona has maintained a high annual sell-through rate of 96%, well above the industry average of 70%. Going forward, the company will continue striving to maintain a high sell-through rate while reducing disposal.

In planning its private-label products, Daytona is working to increase the share of sustainable materials, and in 2019 it launched Firsthand, a concept store themed around sustainable fashion and art. There, it developed original denim items under the “Made-in-Japan Denim” series and used blockchain technology to increase transparency around the companies involved in the production process as well as CO2 emissions generated during production. By accurately measuring CO2 emissions generated in the production process of its standard denim items, Firsthand is working to identify the most effective decarbonization approaches and to further reduce environmental impact in subsequent product planning. By addressing the challenge of “visualizing production processes” in apparel manufacturing—making previously opaque production backgrounds visible and linking them to tamper-proof blockchain data—Firsthand has provided consumers with a new basis for making purchasing decisions.

Beyond Firsthand, the share of products carrying the company-wide sustainable mark\* rose from 6.9% in 2022 to 11.9% in 2023. In addition, its stores collect unwanted clothing for recycling regardless of whether the items are Daytona products or those of other companies, and reuse them as interior materials in stores; to date, 1,650 kg has been put to use in this way. The company also began measuring CO2 emissions from its offices and stores in 2023, and from 2024 it has started measuring emissions associated with products as well, with the goal of visualizing the environmental impact of its operations and reducing it wherever possible.

In addition to business-unit initiatives, a distinctive feature of Daytona’s efforts is its collaborations with local communities, communicated through each store and various other touchpoints.

At its Nagano store, Daytona partnered with “Minna Denryoku,” which uses the catchphrase “electricity with a visible face,” and began offering the renewable energy plan “FREAK’S Electricity.” This initiative simultaneously addresses two challenges: increasing awareness of renewable energy among Daytona’s core young customer base, and utilizing abandoned farmland in Nagano Prefecture. A portion of each month’s electricity bill paid by subscribers to FREAK’S Electricity is donated as support funds to the NPO Shinano Soil, which focuses on social initiatives primarily with students in the prefecture. Daytona also supported a project to turn crops grown on fields cultivated by the NPO into products such as popcorn and beer; the 2023 harvest festival became an event attended by more than 80 participants, including local government officials, students, media, and farmers. Shinano Soil’s planted area has expanded to approximately 2,000 square meters, yielding approximately 200 kg of corn. The popcorn “Bakuretsu Shinano Pop,” born from the FREAK’S Electricity initiative, won the “Happy Shinshu Category Award” at the Shinshu Brand Award 2023, a prefecture-led program in which industry, academia, and government in Nagano collaboratively select and recognize brands and designs that contribute to strengthening Shinshu’s brand power—receiving high praise as a product created through innovation driven by co-creation. Bakuretsu Shinano Pop is a rare domestically produced popcorn cultivated with care in an environmentally conscious manner—without pesticides, herbicides, additives, or genetic modification—and is vacuum-packed at peak freshness, resulting in a product that is also visually enjoyable.

At its Shizuoka store, Daytona launched OSAKANA FREAK, an "Eat, Wear, Learn" SDGs project themed around underutilized fish. Focusing on the global challenges of declining catch volumes and food loss, in May 2023 Daytona worked with universities, research institutions, and local companies in Shizuoka Prefecture to develop new local specialties using underutilized resources. As the first product of this collaboration, it developed and sold "Yaizu Bonito Peperoncino," a pasta sauce made from underutilized parts of bonito that would otherwise be discarded, through FREAK'S STORE locations and events. In January 2024, it launched a second product, "Yaizu Bonito Amatriciana." By working with local communities across industry boundaries, Daytona has communicated the nature, challenges, and appeal of Shizuoka Prefecture to people outside the region. These efforts were highly valued by Shizuoka City, leading first to projects promoting Shizuoka-grown wasabi and tourism resources and eventually to a comprehensive partnership agreement with the city, the first such agreement ever concluded by an apparel company.

Daytona has also concluded a comprehensive partnership agreement with Koga City in Ibaraki Prefecture, where it was founded, and collaborates with the city in various ways. One example is “&FREAK.,” a community-based coworking space that opened in April 2022 by utilizing the second-floor space of The Camp FREAK’S STORE in Koga City. &FREAK. is a coworking space built around the concept “Share a life you can be passionate about.” Beyond serving as a base for business and hobbies, it is also a place where people can hone what they love and create new connections with peers in the local community through activities in its studio and event spaces. Within its first year, 10 companies signed annual contracts, and it has hosted a variety of events such as plant and accessory markets and art exhibitions—creating new flows of people into Koga City.

These collaborations with local communities and local companies and organizations are realized through seamless coordination between the headquarters branding function and stores. President Sasaki of Daytona describes these efforts as follows:

> "Daytona International builds communities with customers through stores, e-commerce, social media, and owned media. Regardless of the type of touchpoint or whether the business is B2C or B2B, we value communication that stays close to each person and brings them joy. Our various initiatives are born from a shared desire among management, employees, customers, and business partners to connect with one another and contribute to society by promoting the excitement and anticipation that make people feel truly engaged."

These diverse collaborations make full use of Daytona's strengths in product planning and communication. With the idea of "toward a prosperous future, living together with everyone" at its core, Daytona will continue pursuing initiatives that engage both consumers and communities.

# Establishing the Circular Economy Growth Capital Fund

Ideation 3X, Unison’s first investment in India, continues to grow steadily as a platform in the circular economy sector, having secured investments from several Japanese companies. This experience has deepened our insights into: 1) the significant gap between supply and demand for growth capital for startups in India, and 2) the increasing willingness of Japanese companies to proactively pursue growth opportunities in the Indian market through partnerships with startups. Based on these insights, Unison co-founded Wildflower Capital with Baring Private Equity Partners India, a firm with extensive investment experience in growth investments in the Indian market. We then launched the Circular Economy Growth Capital Fund (the “Fund”) with the aim of generating significant social impact and investment returns through investments in the circular economy sector.

At the COP26 World Leaders Summit in November 2021, the Indian government pledged to achieve carbon neutrality by 2070 and committed to reaching 500GW from non-fossil fuel sources by 2030. To achieve these goals, various infrastructure projects—such as renewable energy, India’s smart city initiatives, and decarbonization projects—are progressing. Advancing these infrastructure projects requires Indian companies with technologies in areas related to the 4R model (Reduce, Reuse, Recycle, Redesign). Investment in the circular economy sector is essential to achieving carbon neutrality in India and holds a key role in enabling sustainable growth of the Indian economy.

The Fund aims to connect Japanese companies’ 4R technologies in the circular economy sector with growth opportunities in India and, as a result, introduce advanced Japanese technologies into India’s low-cost, technology-based business models to create globally scalable solutions in the circular economy sector. Japanese companies that possess advanced solutions in the 4R domain and are seeking to newly develop the Indian market position India as a growth opportunity and are looking for local partners with deep knowledge of India’s industrial structure. Japanese companies seeking to diversify their supply chains from a geopolitical perspective will be able to access global solutions in India’s circular economy sector through an investment in the Fund.

The Fund will provide growth capital—such as Series B and C—to Indian startups with 4R-related technologies, while also investing growth capital needed by established small and mid-sized companies to scale solutions in the circular economy sector. In addition, in making these investments, we will actively pursue co-investments with Japanese companies that have strengths in the 4R domain.

The Fund’s investment activities also align with various policies promoted by the Indian government to drive adoption of the circular economy. For example, the Indian government has adopted a policy package called Clean India Mission 2.0, an initiative to introduce an Integrated Solid Waste Management (ISWM) model across India. It is also linked to policy packages such as Mission LiFE, which aims to implement sustainable lifestyles by introducing the 4R model across industrial sectors through laws known as Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR). Working in tandem with these policies, the Fund will generate significant impact for Indian society.

This project is a new challenge within Unison’s sustainability investing, to which we have remained disciplined and committed since our founding. Formal marketing of the Fund will begin shortly. The Fund aims to connect India and Japan and foster new collaboration and innovation.

# Afterword

This year marks the fourth edition of Unison's Sustainability Report, first published in 2021. Since our founding, Unison has been committed to a business purpose that goes beyond mere financial gain, encompassing social impact and value creation.

This year's report focuses on our consumer sector practice, which has continuously evolved over the 25 years since the establishment of Fund I. As demonstrated by the initiatives of Pyuru and Daytona International, we view ESG not merely as a set of rules or obligations, but as an integral part of our core business. Each portfolio company has implemented concrete measures aimed at benefiting local communities, employees, and the environment.

As these examples illustrate, our goal is to help the companies we invest in achieve both long-term enhancement of corporate value and fulfillment of their social responsibilities. To objectively assess whether these initiatives are also delivering measurable contributions to society, we have co-developed a sustainability visualization initiative with RIMM and have participated in the EDCI project, among other efforts, with the aim of embedding best practices across our entire portfolio.

This report also features photographs of wildflowers taken by Rahul Bhasin, a founding member of Wildflower Capital. It also includes several photographs I took at a botanical garden in Karuizawa.

Through this report, we hope to share our ESG initiatives with you in a more concrete and tangible way, while also using it as a foundation for discussion on what ESG should look like within the private equity business model. We welcome your feedback and comments.
