# Unison's ESG Initiatives and Framework

Since its founding, Unison has pursued impact and social value beyond financial profit as a core business objective. This is clearly stated in the Guiding Principles that express our founding philosophy.

> While we rigorously pursue profitable opportunities, we aim to make an impact on the business community we are involved in.
>
> We seek to create business transactions which have value not only to ourselves but also to society.

Based on this philosophy, we have consistently implemented initiatives since Fund I, believing that measures such as improving employees' working environments and morale at portfolio companies contribute to their long-term performance and corporate value. As ESG initiatives have come to be emphasized in the private equity industry, we established ESG policies in 2016 and have made disclosures as a PRI signatory since 2017. In 2020, we also defined Unison's own ESG framework and began rolling it out to portfolio companies. Today, starting with efforts to visualize impact, we hope in the future to quantitatively demonstrate that our ESG initiatives have a positive effect on investment returns.

We believe that private equity investment is an optimal governance model for advancing ESG initiatives. As control investors, we are in a position to realize the ESG agenda we pursue within portfolio companies, and we recognize that we bear a heavier responsibility than investors in listed equities. Particularly among the small and medium-sized companies targeted by Unison, many companies lag in developing organizational and facility infrastructure relative to their business scale, and improving these areas is directly linked to improved performance and corporate value. We also believe that pursuing the kind of company that is highly valued by investors as a listed company, or highly valued by a listed company considering bringing it into its group as a subsidiary, not only from a business and financial perspective but also in relation to society and the environment, leads to positive outcomes at exit. ESG initiatives remain a work in progress among Japanese private equity funds, but Unison participated in the launch of the ESG Committee of the Japan Private Equity Association and actively plans study sessions and best-practice sharing among GPs, helping to advance initiatives across the industry.

## Two-Stage CDE Framework and Monitoring Approach

Unison presents its ESG initiatives, premised on control investments in small and medium-sized companies, through the following two-stage framework and rolls them out to each portfolio company.

### [Stage 1] Community, Diversity & Empowerment

- C: Contribution to the local communities and customer communities to which the company belongs

- D: Ensuring employee diversity. Increasing the ratio of women in managerial and higher positions. Creating a skills matrix for the board of directors and actively inviting outside directors from outside Unison

- E: Improving employees' working environments and implementing appropriate evaluation and treatment. Empowerment through information sharing, etc.

### [Stage 2] Carbon, Disposables & Energy

- C: Reducing carbon dioxide emissions through business activities

- D: Reducing the use of plastic and other materials with high environmental burden

- E: Improving efficiency in energy consumption, including water. Actively adopting renewable energy, etc.

For Unison's portfolio companies, which are not necessarily large in scale, making visible improvements in environmental impact from the outset can be organizationally challenging. We first work with them to design and implement Stage 1 initiatives, where explicit improvements can be made with their own hands. This lowers the hurdle for ESG initiatives and connects them to measures that management teams can more readily feel are effective, such as talent acquisition and employee retention. Once the organization has experienced a certain level of results, we then proceed to Stage 2 initiatives centered on environmental impact.

For both Stage 1 and Stage 2, specific initiatives are set for each portfolio company in line with its mission statement and investment thesis, and in accordance with its business scale and organizational situation. In formulating the initiatives to be undertaken, we set targets and milestones for each portfolio company while referring to materiality and best practices in each industry. By incorporating this process into the formulation of business plans immediately after investment and the review of business plans during the investment period, and by adding it to management's annual qualitative evaluation, we monitor these initiatives on an ongoing basis in the same way as business KPIs.

## Visualization of Impact through Quantification

Unison quantifies and accumulates data on ESG initiatives through the following methods.

### RIMM Sustainability

To visualize the ESG initiatives of portfolio companies, Unison co-developed a platform for GPs with RIMM Sustainability ("RIMM").

RIMM states its business purpose as "making sustainability simple, accessible, fun, and duly rewarding for companies of all sizes." Specifically, it provides a software platform service that visualizes, quantifies, and evaluates corporate sustainability initiatives in accordance with international standards such as the Global Reporting Initiative (GRI) and the Sustainability Accounting Standards Board (SASB).

RIMM's platform combines information on sustainability initiatives provided by the companies being evaluated with sustainability information on global listed and private companies incorporated into RIMM's database, using a proprietary algorithm. It visualizes the evaluated company's initiatives in the form of a "RIMM Score" and an "SDGs Score." The feedback report also shows current status and areas for improvement together with case studies of peer companies, and is designed to lead to concrete action.

The GP dashboard enables users to compare each portfolio company's responses and benchmark results side by side in a single view. For KPIs where initiatives are lagging, it helps users dig into the causes and encourage the adoption of best practices from other portfolio companies, thereby helping to advance initiatives across the portfolio.

In addition to Unison's unique two-stage CDE framework, conducting third-party evaluation and benchmarking against peer companies through RIMM assessments has helped ensure that each portfolio company's ESG initiatives are not self-satisfied, and that they evolve with awareness of the materiality considered important in each industry. RIMM's platform also supports the automatic creation of sustainability reports based on data accumulated over time, making it available for use by portfolio companies that plan IPOs or external disclosure in the future.

### ESG Data Convergence Initiative

Unison is the first Japanese GP to participate in the ESG Data Convergence Initiative ("EDCI") and provides data every year.

EDCI is a framework in which approximately 450 GPs and LPs, including major global PE firms and asset managers, collaborate and agree to collect and report key ESG indicators in a standardized manner. It is the first PE industry-led initiative to standardize GP disclosure indicators and create a database that enables performance comparison.

Prior to participating in EDCI, Unison began measuring GHG emissions across all portfolio companies. Some companies with their own production facilities had already measured Scope 1 direct emissions and Scope 2 indirect emissions, but for most portfolio companies this was their first attempt to measure GHG emissions. When companies were unable to make conversions in-house, we introduced external emissions measurement services and turned direct and indirect emissions into data. The measurement results were benchmarked through both RIMM and EDCI, but concrete initiatives to reduce emissions are still to come, and in the future we will also need to expand our efforts to understand Scope 3 emissions, including emissions in the supply chain.

EDCI benchmarks the collected data against listed companies, and while companies under PE ownership are seen to lag at the starting point, it has been confirmed that the degree of improvement and change during the investment period exceeds that of listed companies. The EDCI initiative quantitatively demonstrates the power of PE funds making control investments to drive organizational change, and enables the track records of each GP to be confirmed using the same definitions. In doing so, it ensures transparency in ESG disclosure, where greenwashing is a constant concern.

Sustainability initiatives are not temporary. They are activities that each company should continue to upgrade even after Unison exits its investment. Using quantification and benchmarking as materials, we repeatedly discuss these issues with the managers of portfolio companies and advance our daily activities so that each company's initiatives become sustainable.

# ESG Initiatives in the Consumer Sector

Since the time of Fund I, Unison has invested in a variety of consumer goods and multi-location businesses, and the themes of supporting these portfolio companies and enhancing their value include the prototype of Unison's ESG approach.

For example, Tohato, a long-established confectionery manufacturer in Fund I for which we supported Japan's first prepackaged civil rehabilitation, created a brand book for employees titled "The Happiness of Making Sweets Your Work" during the process of rebuilding the company. It was a pop-up book created to communicate "the happiness of making what you love your work" and "the meaning of working as a team," and to share intent. The phrase "Let's become a team, rather than merely an organization," coined by Hidetoshi Nakata, who became Chief Branding Officer, supported the organization as a spiritual pillar of management reform.

Akindo Sushiro, a Fund II portfolio company that became No. 1 in customer satisfaction in the restaurant industry, grew during our investment period from the third-largest to the largest conveyor-belt sushi chain in Japan. This rapid growth was supported by an "inverted pyramid organization with stores at the top." Rather than issuing top-down commands, management clearly indicated that the head office's job was to thoroughly support the frontline staff who interact with customers every day, and improved various company systems, including personnel systems and labor management.

The consumer sector spans a wide range of industries, and each subsector has depth, so a "one size fits all" approach does not work. Unison currently measures and quantifies common ESG indicators across all portfolio companies, but in the consumer sector we place particular emphasis on building ESG approaches that fit each portfolio company's business model and needs. In the following pages, we introduce initiatives at Pyuru and Daytona International as specific examples of Unison's approach of working closely with portfolio companies.

## Pyuru: Pursuing Effective Clean Beauty

Pyuru, in which Unison invested in June 2019, is engaged end-to-end in the production, manufacturing, and sale of hair care products and skincare cosmetics using natural ingredients. Since its founding in 1998, the company has focused on developing and manufacturing natural brand products that are gentle on hair and skin while offering strong hair-coloring effects, as represented by the Rishiri Hair Color Series, a gray hair dye hair treatment brand that has held the No. 1 sales position in Japan for 13 consecutive years. The new management team, which took over business operations from the founder who retired upon the transfer of shares to Unison, formulated a more modernized mission and vision while preserving the founding spirit.

Since its founding, Pyuru has developed products using natural ingredients, and its unique concept of Effective Clean Beauty articulates this philosophy. The mission and vision formulated based on this concept articulate Pyuru's commitment to supporting beauty beyond age and to providing globally competitive, highly effective, environmentally friendly products. These define the axis of Pyuru's brand development, R&D, and ESG initiatives, and have become the driving force for the next stage of growth. President Inoue describes the importance of maintaining the founder's values while updating the company for its next stage and establishing a common language for the organization as follows:

"For many years, we have stood alongside people facing gray hair, which is consistently one of the most common concerns about hair. By steadily pursuing the initiatives set out in our mission and vision, we have grown into a brand that has achieved the No. 1 sales position in Japan for 13 consecutive years and continues to be chosen by many customers. In recent years, we have also launched new brands that address concerns among younger generations, such as premature gray hair, hair loss after COVID-19 or childbirth, and scalp concerns among younger people who enjoy fashion. Going forward, we will continue aiming to develop products that address the concerns of many people, which can lead to complexes, and that help solve social issues. Furthermore, to ensure that all of our products continue to be chosen over the long term, we will continue to realize Effective Clean Beauty based on our desire to deliver products that are not only gentle on people and the environment, but are also truly effective."

### Commitment to Raw Materials and Environmental Initiatives in Manufacturing and Development Processes

With its mission and vision clarified, Pyuru began working to embody the philosophy behind them. Its head office factory, newly established in 2017, is located in Itoshima City, Fukuoka Prefecture, an area rich in nature, and enables the company to control and manage much of its manufacturing process and energy consumption in-house. Even today, Pyuru uses natural energy such as solar and wind power and covers approximately 10% of its electricity needs with renewable energy. It also implements energy-saving measures, such as controlling air-conditioning equipment and the number of air compressors in operation according to usage, and plans to further shift to renewable energy. Through these activities, Pyuru aims to achieve net-zero greenhouse gas emissions by 2026.

As an activity to reduce the burden on the global environment, Pyuru has achieved zero emissions of waste by sorting and recycling raw material containers and cardboard boxes generated in production processes, as well as waste generated in offices. In addition, at its manufacturing site, the company has built a production system capable of complying with ECOCERT COSMOS certification,\* a global organic certification standard. By accumulating daily improvements based on opinions and suggestions from each person involved in production, Pyuru is updating its manufacturing processes to align with global environmental standards.

Pyuru has also established its own standards for raw materials and packaging used in its products. For example, it does not use ingredients such as microplastics, phthalates, parabens, mineral oil, and sulfate-based surfactants. In addition, RISHIRIA Furel, a new product targeting younger generations from a sister brand of the Rishiri Series, has succeeded in reducing plastic use by approximately 70% through innovations in its refill containers. Furthermore, Pyuru has partnered with local farms to establish its own farm, where it cultivates herbs and plants used in its products. Employees participate in farm work and deepen their involvement with the local community.

When developing new products, Pyuru sets principles aligned with Effective Clean Beauty.

- Use ingredients that are considerate of both the body and the environment

- Emphasize scientific evidence

- Continue listening to and engaging with customers' voices

- Thoroughly localize across countries, environments, and cultures

LIHAW, one of the new brands launched in 2021, uses fragrances extracted from plants and herbs grown on Pyuru's own farm, and provides high-quality, effective skincare cosmetics at affordable prices. Wicot, a line for wholesale channels, is Japan's first beauty serum certified under ECOCERT COSMOS, and is also developed and manufactured in-house.

### Regional Job Creation and a Fair Evaluation System

Many women work actively at Pyuru's head office and factory, contributing to job creation in the Itoshima region. As part-time employees have increasingly been promoted to full-time positions through revisions to the personnel evaluation system, the ratio of women in management positions has steadily increased since Unison's investment. Pyuru also encourages men to take childcare leave, and since 2020 the rate of childcare leave taken by male employees has been 100%. The company is also advancing initiatives to share internally the joy of being involved in hair care products and skincare cosmetics, such as makeup classes for employees.

"An employee-friendly working environment is created by implementing initiatives that are not bound by organizational customs or fixed ideas, that fairly evaluate people regardless of gender, age, years of service, or other factors, and that lead to a better work-life balance. The promotion of women to management positions and childcare leave taken by male employees are examples of this," says CFO Tominaga. Specific initiatives include the introduction of four types of surveys, such as a 360-degree survey in which employees write what they would like colleagues to "continue doing more of" or "change," and a "requests from subordinates to supervisors" survey, as well as personnel interviews conducted twice a year. Management reviews all of this content and provides timely feedback to respondents and takes improvement actions. As a human resources policy, Pyuru will continue to remove noise from evaluations, such as past experience and achievements or gender, and pursue the creation of an environment in which employees who are highly motivated to demonstrate competencies and committed to results can thrive.

### Conclusion

The formulation of Pyuru's mission, vision, and subsequent ESG policy clarified the value that Pyuru provides through its business activities and how that value connects with the world, while remaining faithful to its founding philosophy. It has built a solid foundation for the company's next stage of growth. Becoming a company chosen by consumers and employees is closely linked with ESG initiatives, and we expect Pyuru to further develop its own initiatives even after the end of Unison's investment period.

## Daytona International: Planning Capabilities that Engage Communities from the Front Lines

Daytona International, in which Unison invested in April 2021, operates an apparel retail business in the form of select shops. Centered on its core business, FREAK'S STORE, the company has established a unique worldview and positioning, and continues to expand its business in the lifestyle domain with fashion at its core. Daytona's success is often discussed in the context of DX/OMO, with e-commerce sales exceeding 60% of total sales. However, to gain support from generations with high environmental awareness in the apparel industry, which is generally said to have a high environmental burden, the company has set the goal of "toward a rich future, living together with everyone" and focuses on the following four areas.

- Community: Maximize and communicate the appeal of local regions

- Value Chain: Responsible action by everyone

- People: Enable diverse individuality to shine

- Environment: Pass on a rich Earth to the next generation

Among its environmental initiatives, Daytona has set the goal of becoming a "company that finishes things well," aiming to eliminate waste throughout the value chain and create a new circular economy. Daytona is directly addressing the issue of product disposal, an industry practice that poses major challenges both in terms of environmental burden and business performance. Through more precise demand forecasting and agile sales that respond to customer needs, Daytona sells through the products it plans and maintains a high annual product sell-through rate of 96%, far above the industry average of 70%. Going forward, the company will aim to maintain this high sell-through rate and continue working to reduce disposal.

In planning its own-brand products, Daytona is working to increase the ratio of sustainable materials. In 2019, it launched Firsthand, a concept store themed around sustainable fashion and art. There, it developed original denim items under the Made in Japan Denim Series and used blockchain technology to make transparent the companies involved in the production process and the CO2 emissions generated in that process. By accurately understanding the CO2 emissions generated in the production process for Firsthand's standard denim products, the company is working to identify the optimal approach to decarbonization and further reduce environmental burden in its next product planning. By approaching the challenge of "visualizing the production process" in apparel manufacturing, it visualized production backgrounds that had previously been difficult to see and linked them to tamper-proof blockchain, providing consumers with a new selection criterion.

Not limited to Firsthand, the ratio of products assigned the Sustainable Mark,\* which Daytona introduced company-wide in 2022, rose from 6.9% in 2022 to 11.9% in 2023. In addition, stores collect unwanted clothing regardless of whether it is their own or other companies' products, recycle it, and reuse it as interior materials for stores. To date, 1,650 kg has been utilized. In 2023, Daytona began measuring CO2 emissions from its own offices and stores, and in 2024 it also began understanding emissions related to products, visualizing the environmental burden of its business activities and aiming to reduce it as much as possible.

In addition to initiatives at the business-unit level, a distinctive feature of Daytona's efforts is collaboration with local communities communicated through each store and various touchpoints.

At the Nagano store, Daytona collaborated with Minna Denryoku, whose catchphrase is "electricity with a face," and began selling the renewable electricity plan FREAK'S Electricity. This initiative simultaneously addresses two issues: raising awareness of renewable energy among Daytona's main customers, who are younger generations, and utilizing abandoned farmland in Nagano Prefecture. A portion of the monthly electricity fees paid by FREAK'S Electricity subscribers is donated as support money to Shinano Soil, an NPO engaged in social activities mainly by students enrolled in schools in the prefecture. Daytona also supports Shinano Soil's plan to commercialize products such as popcorn and beer from fields cultivated by the NPO, and the 2023 harvest festival became an event attended by more than 80 people, including government officials, students, media, and farmers. Shinano Soil's cultivated area has increased to approximately 2,000 square meters, and it has harvested about 200 kg of corn. Bakuretsu Shinano Pop, the popcorn born from FREAK'S Electricity activities, won the Happy Shinshu Category Award at the Shinshu Brand Award 2023, a prefectural award in which Nagano's industry, academia, and government jointly select and honor brands and designs that have contributed to enhancing the Shinshu brand. It has been highly evaluated as a product created through co-creation-driven innovation. Bakuretsu Shinano Pop is carefully grown as rare domestic popcorn without pesticides, herbicides, additives, or genetic modification, with consideration for the environment, and the freshly harvested corn is vacuum-packed into a visually enjoyable product.

At the Shizuoka store, Daytona planned OSAKANA FREAK, an "Eat, Wear, and Learn SDGs Project" themed around underutilized fish. Focusing on the global issues of declining fish catches and food loss, in May 2023 the store worked with universities, research institutions, and local companies in Shizuoka Prefecture to develop new specialty products using underutilized resources. As the first jointly developed product, it developed Yaizu Bonito Peperoncino, a pasta sauce made from underutilized parts of bonito that would otherwise be discarded, and sells it at FREAK'S STORE locations and events. In January 2024, the second product, Yaizu Bonito Amatriciana, was launched. Crossing industry boundaries, the initiative has communicated the nature, challenges, and appeal of Shizuoka Prefecture beyond the prefecture together with the local community. The initiative was highly evaluated by Shizuoka City, leading to a project to promote Shizuoka-produced wasabi and tourism resources, and developed into a comprehensive partnership agreement with Shizuoka City, the first such agreement for an apparel company.

Daytona has also concluded a comprehensive partnership agreement with Koga City in Ibaraki Prefecture, its place of founding, and collaborates with the city in various ways. One example is &FREAK., a community-type coworking space opened in April 2022 using the second-floor space of The Camp FREAK'S STORE in Koga City. &FREAK. is a coworking space based on the concept of "Share a life you can be passionate about." It is not only a base for business and hobbies, but also a place where people can refine what they love through activities in the studio and event space and connect with new companions in the local community. In its first year of operation, 10 companies signed annual contracts, and it also held various events such as plant and accessory sales events and art exhibitions, creating a new flow of people into Koga City.

These collaborations with local communities and local companies and organizations are realized through seamless cooperation between the head office branding department and stores. President Sasaki of Daytona describes these initiatives as follows:

"Daytona International forms communities with customers through stores, e-commerce, social media, owned media, and other channels. Regardless of the type of touchpoint or the form of business, such as BtoC or BtoB, we value bringing joy to everyone through communication that stays close to them. Our various initiatives are also born from a shared desire among management, employees, customers, and partners: by promoting the excitement and fun that make people thrilled, we connect with one another and want to contribute to society."

Daytona's diverse collaborations are initiatives that make use of its product planning and communication capabilities. Based on the idea of "toward a rich future, living together with everyone," Daytona will continue initiatives that engage consumers and communities.

# Establishment of the Circular Economy Growth Capital Fund

Ideation 3X, Unison's first investment project in India, has received investments from multiple Japanese companies and continues to grow steadily as a platform in the circular economy field. Through this experience, we have deepened our understanding that 1) there is a significant supply-demand gap for growth capital for startups in India, and 2) Japanese companies are increasingly willing to actively pursue growth opportunities in the Indian market through partnerships with startups. Based on these insights, Unison co-founded Wildflower Capital with Baring Private Equity Partners India, which has extensive investment experience in growth investment in the Indian market. We then formed the Circular Economy Growth Capital Fund (the "New Fund"), aiming to generate significant social impact and investment returns through investment activities in the circular economy field.

At the COP26 World Leaders Summit in November 2021, the Indian government pledged to achieve carbon neutrality by 2070 and committed to sourcing 500 GW from non-fossil fuels by 2030. To achieve this, various infrastructure projects are progressing, including renewable energy, India-version smart cities, and decarbonization projects. The advancement of these infrastructure projects requires the presence of Indian companies with technologies in areas related to the 4R model (Reduce, Reuse, Recycle, Redesign). Investment in the circular economy field is indispensable to achieving carbon neutrality in India and holds a major key to enabling the Indian economy to achieve sustainable growth.

The New Fund aims to connect Japanese companies' 4R technologies in the circular economy field with growth opportunities in India. As a result, it aims to introduce Japan's advanced technologies into business models based on India's low-cost technologies and create circular economy solutions that can be deployed globally. Japanese companies that possess advanced solutions in the 4R areas and seek to newly develop the Indian market view the Indian market as a growth opportunity and are looking for local partners familiar with India's industrial structure. Japanese companies seeking to diversify their supply chains from a geopolitical perspective will be able to access global solutions in India's circular economy field through investment in the New Fund.

The New Fund will provide Series B, Series C, and other growth capital to Indian startups with technologies related to the 4Rs, and will also invest growth capital needed by small and medium-sized companies with operating histories to scale solutions in the circular economy field. In making these investments, the New Fund will actively pursue co-investments with Japanese companies that have strengths in the 4R field.

The New Fund's investment activities are also aligned with various policies regarding the introduction of the circular economy promoted by the Indian government. For example, the Indian government has adopted a policy package called Clean India Mission 2.0, an initiative to introduce an integrated waste-treatment model called Integrated Solid Waste Management (ISWM) in India. It is also linked to a policy package called Mission LiFE, which aims to introduce the 4R model into each industrial sector through a law called Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR) and implement sustainable lifestyles. The New Fund will create significant impact in Indian society while aligning with these various policies.

This project is a new challenge in investment in sustainability, to which Unison has remained committed with discipline since its founding. Formal marketing of the New Fund will begin shortly. The New Fund aims to connect India and Japan and create new collaboration and innovation.

# Afterword

Unison's Sustainability Report, first published in 2021, marks its fourth edition this year. Since its founding, Unison has placed the pursuit of impact and social value beyond financial profit at the center of its business.

This year's report focuses on our consumer practice, which has continued to evolve for approximately 25 years since Fund I. As you can see from the initiatives of Pyuru and Daytona International, we view ESG not merely as rules or obligations, but as something that starts from its connection to the core business. Each portfolio company is implementing concrete measures aimed at considering and contributing to local communities, employees, and the environment.

As these cases make clear, our goal is to help the companies we are involved with through investment achieve both the long-term enhancement of corporate value and the fulfillment of social responsibility. In addition, to objectively evaluate whether such measures are also quantitatively linked to contributions to society, we have developed initiatives with RIMM to visualize sustainability and participated in the EDCI project, aiming to ensure that industry-wide best practices permeate all portfolio companies.

This report also introduces photographs of wildflowers taken by Rahul Bhasin, a founding member of Wildflower Capital. It also includes several photographs that I took myself at a botanical garden in Karuizawa.

Through this report, we hope to introduce ESG initiative examples to all of you in a more concrete and tangible way, and to use it as a foundation for discussing what ESG should be in the private equity business model. We look forward to receiving many of your opinions and impressions.
