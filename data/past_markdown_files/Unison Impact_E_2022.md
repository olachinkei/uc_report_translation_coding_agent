# Interview with RIMM Sustainability

**Junko:** It is great to see you in person finally after countless Zoom calls these past few years. Firstly, will you tell us why you started RIMM Sustainability (“RIMM”) and what you are trying to achieve? 

**Ravi:** My interest in sustainability started back during my days in investment banking. Being faced with financial capitalism every day, I grew to be aware of the inequities and damage that high finance does. Over the years, I came to know of people who were involved in sustainability start-ups, and I sat on a few boards of such companies. I started a teaching gig at Yale-NUS College after gaining all this experience and created a curriculum surrounding sustainability. It just felt natural to start a business venture on it. RIMM is a culmination of my life experiences, essentially. 

**Junko:** I believe Osamu met you back in 2019. We had been looking for a good reporting tool as we could not find any existing service meeting mid-cap GPs’ needs and decided to invest in RIMM’s seed round. Ever since, we have been working together as one team to develop a GP-oriented assessment and the Fund Manager Dashboard. During these times, how did your team develop, what is the role of your Scientific Board and what is the plan for future? 

**Ravi:** Our team has grown significantly over the past few years. We currently have around 25 employees and a growing tech and product team to keep up with customer demands. We also have a strong research and education team, who makes sure that RIMM is staying updated with sustainability trends. 

The Scientific Board consists of world-class experts in various fields such as finance, sustainability, and environmental science. Dr. Kim Schumacher, one of Unison’s advisors, is a member of the board. The Scientific Board has two main roles. The first is to assist RIMM in evaluating, reviewing and contributing to the thought leadership of the company, especially in relation to ESG data analytics and environmental data. There will also be a special focus on cutting-edge carbon inventory and mitigation models, which will be shared with clients. The second is to maintain a curated list of scientific experts that will assist RIMM and RIMM’s clients by providing advice and scientifically backed verification on their sustainability reporting or any other related initiatives. 

We are aiming to make a widespread impact. Right now, we are focusing on co-development opportunities to boost RIMM’s ability to help all types of companies. We are developing comprehensive sustainability solutions for large enterprise to address sustainability needs within their network. These large enterprises, such as financial institutions and insurance companies, often have a large SME network and we work with them to develop solutions for these SMEs. We create a holistic, end-to-end journey that enables enterprises and their network to achieve their sustainability goals. With Unison’s support, we established a Japanese corporation, and in March of this year we were selected to participate in the “Green Finance Subsidy Program for Tokyo Market Entry”. We are working with several financial institutions and business corporations on implementing RIMM’s services, and in future will look to expand this type of offering along with products intended for fund managers to other countries.

**Junko:** When I first took on ESG responsibilities at Unison, I was overwhelmed by the number of different disclosure requirements. While RIMM is primarily referring to the Global Reporting Initiative (GRI) and the Sustainability Accounting Standards Board (SASB), other entities and government bodies are also trying to set different disclosure standards. Could you share your insight with us on how you see the future of this “standard setting war” and potential convergence?

**Ravi:** To start us off from the very beginning, GRI is one of the very first sustainability reporting standards that was created and is the most commonly used one currently worldwide. It is an industry-agnostic reporting standard that is currently in the process of developing recommended sector-specific sustainability disclosures. 

As for SASB, it is viewed from investor and financial perspectives, such as what ESG criteria are important for the financial performance of a company. There is also the Task Force on Climate-related Financial Disclosures (TCFD), which was created by the Financial Stability Board (FSB) at the request of the G20, and is currently a fast-growing standard in Japan and the rest of the world as it considers climate risks and their impact on financial performance. 

It has been estimated that there are around 600 environment, social and governance reporting standards globally, often described as an ‘alphabet soup’ of sustainability standards. They mainly consist of voluntary disclosure regimes and initiatives that have surfaced over the last couple of years especially with the increasing recognition of the importance of ESG. However, the multitude of standards present also poses various challenges for companies - they lack meaningful guidance as to which reporting frameworks and standards to follow. Shareholders and investors expect boards to be fluent in sustainability, which is difficult to do without a common language. 

However, recently, some standards have been undergoing convergence. The Value Reporting Foundation, which operates several standards including SASB, will consolidate all of the standards under the IFRS Foundation, which is currently establishing the new International Sustainability Standards Board (ISSB) to develop a unified international standard for company ESG disclosures.

**Junko:** Lastly, as a mid-cap GP, where do you think Unison can make a difference? How should we communicate our progress with our stakeholders?

**Ravi:** Unison, as a private equity fund, has a strong responsibility to be a sustainable steward and owner of these companies from an ESG perspective. Ownership allows Unison to implement ESG bottom-up at all portfolio companies. Implementing ESG will reduce exit risks from due diligence, create genuine value for the company, as well as create an impact on society as measured by SDGs. Establishing robust communication channels and involving your stakeholders in frequent progress updates is key.

## Progress on the GP-oriented sustainability platform

We have continued our co-development partnership with RIMM on building a GP-oriented sustainability platform. In this section, we introduce the latest developments and future goals.

RIMM’s platform provides Unison’s portfolio companies with assessments customized by industry and based on international standards including GRI and SASB, resulting in scoring reports that includes competitor benchmarks. For GPs, RIMM provides a dashboard that enables GPs to view and compare portfolio companies’ responses and benchmark results.

With RIMM’s scoring reports, portfolio companies can now evaluate their sustainability efforts from an industry-level perspective and check their progress through regular assessments. Through competitive benchmarks, portfolio companies can understand what activities and efforts are being furthered in their respective industries, and better identify and review focus areas. While RIMM currently refers to global database Refinitiv to benchmark portfolio companies against listed companies in the appropriate industry, plans are in place to aggregate data from private companies by offering RIMM’s assessment to a wider audience and in future build a proprietary database to allow more detailed benchmarking. By enabling this type of competitor benchmarking, we believe portfolio companies can better identify how society evaluates specific sustainability efforts as well as optimize their sustainability goals to tie in with value creation.

On a final note, Unison’s commitment to building an internal, specialized GP-oriented platform is due to the achievement gaps that we see arising when small to mid-cap companies’ sustainability efforts rely on existing external assessment services. Unison’s experience with small to mid-cap companies has taught us that these companies require both realistic and repeatable methods to achieve their crucial role in improving sustainability for society at large. ## N.Field interview: at-home care supporting transition to community life

Foreword: The concept of “integrated regional care system”, systems constructed to have local communities lead medical, nursing and community support services, was codified into law in 2004 as a pillar of Japan’s social security system. Relevant activities have accelerated since 2017, when system coverage expanded from not only Japan’s elderly population but to also those with mental health conditions and neurological diseases. As a response to these reforms, Unison has looked to expand CNS-related investment activities while continuing its focus on building infrastructure to support patient lives through its healthcare investments. This section explores the actions various medical service businesses are taking to increase industrywide partnerships, and considers what the future may hold for the CNS field in Japan.

Tatsunari Morimoto (N.Field CEO)

Hajime Nakamura (N.Field Business Management Department, Public Relations Director, Certified Nurse Specialist in Psychiatric Mental Health Nursing)

x

Eri Kawana (Unison Capital Investor Solutions Team)

**Eri:** With the move to build regional integrated care systems across Japan, the industry is seeing more instances of hospitals, pharmacies and nursing services building alliances or partnerships. How has this presented itself in the CNS field and for N.Field, which provides at-home nursing services to patients with mental health conditions and neurological diseases?

**Tatsunari:** With the expanding aging population, we have seen a jump in medical institutions and at-home nursing operators offering at-home nursing services. The concept of regional care systems was introduced in order to further this trend of “shifting from hospitalization-centered treatment to regional care-centered treatment” in the CNS field. This principle was included in Japan’s Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare’s 2004 “Vision of Mental Health and Medical Welfare Reform”, and its implementation has slowly but surely progressed, with a proactive push in recent years to enable patients with mental health conditions and neurological disorders to receive at-home care. The most recent data published in 2017 by the Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare show that there are roughly four million people in Japan with a mental health condition or neurological disorder and that approximately 30,000 are hospitalized for treatment. Partnerships and alliances among psychiatric hospitals and services like N. Field have begun in order to enable these 30,000 patients to live and receive care at home.

**Eri:** Are these patients able to smoothly be discharged and reintegrate into their communities?

**Tatsunari:** Patients often faced issues with their families or neighbors prior to their hospitalization, and with societal understanding around these conditions slow to progress, many patients’ families are left with a significant burden once patients are discharged. Many things need to be prepared to enable patients to live at home, including but not limited to determining their living situation and support system as they prepare to be independent. On the other hand, awareness around some conditions such as ADHD have grown, with opportunities to better understand these conditions, such as through people openly sharing their diagnosis and conditions on SNS. Japanese society’s understanding and acceptance of mental health conditions and neurological diseases are slowly growing, as are the breadth and depth of social services on offer to support patients.

**Hajime：**It is not unusual for patients with mental health conditions and neurological disorders to spend years hospitalized, particularly when the patients’ communities do not fully understand the nature of these conditions. “Social hospitalization” is a term referring to this phenomenon, where patients not requiring medical treatment continue to be hospitalized. From the 2000s, hospitals were provided economic incentives by the government to discharge patients earlier, specifically within three months following hospitalization. Coupled with the advancement of pharmaceutical treatment options, concepts associated with hospital discharge support and integrated care gradually prevailed. It is only recently that the integrated regional care system has shifted towards catering not only to the elderly, but patients undergoing social hospitalization as well. Despite this progress, building sufficient social infrastructure to enable earlier hospital discharges remains a challenge. In addition to economic incentives for hospitals, the “Vision of Mental Health and Medical Welfare Reform” from 2004 that Tatsunari mentioned earlier outlines goals to reduce CNS-related hospitals by 70,000 beds, but as of 2016 the achieved reduction was only 18,000, the equivalent of 36,000 hospitalized patients. It shows that incentives for medical institutions inevitably fall short when the regional care systems meant to intake these patients remain weak. I also felt this challenge first-hand when I previously worked at a closed ward of a hospital, and joined N.Field due to a desire to better support hospitalization discharges.

**Eri:** Specifically, what functions and services does N.Field provide for addressing this challenge?

**Hajime：** For patients who do not have a place to return to after being discharged, we provide a home for patients following hospital discharge by subleasing residential properties. Furthermore, as part of our at-home nursing services, we work with nurses, psychiatric social workers and occupational therapists to provide the care needed with regard to medication counseling, general livelihood support and external communication required for patients to return to work, school or their communities. Our comprehensive scope of at-home nursing services is intended to cater uniquely to the needs of our patients for them to recover most effectively. We emphasize and respect patients’ independence and work to build a flexible system around them that best supports their needs.

**Tatsunari：**Our evolution is still mid-way. As an exciting next step, we are planning to expand homes supporting independent living as a realistic solution for patients that cannot live alone immediately after hospital discharge. Specifically, these homes provide the training and adjustment required for patients to eventually live alone. As CNS-related disorders often recur, flexible transitions between these homes, hospitals, and living alone are considered optimal for patients. With patients’ doctors kept up to date on a patient’s progress following hospital discharge, rehospitalization can also take place smoothly when necessary. We strongly believe that these homes will contribute significantly to advancing integrated regional care catered to CNS patients.\
**Eri:** It sounds like implementation of the infrastructure required for providing integrated regional care towards CNS patients has made meaningful progress already.

**Tatsunari：**While the work around institutions’ infrastructure is underway, there is still more to be done around better connecting with local communities. There is still a shortage of personnel responsible for supporting communities. At-home nursing presents more obstacles to hiring as nurses are required to provide more individual care versus nursing as part of a team at a hospital ward. The job also requires expertise in the CNS field, making talent recruitment a challenge.

**Hajime：**Hospitals that are aligned with facilities/services like ourselves tend to more proactively undertake hospital discharge support activities. Hence, it is important for us to strengthen our network with hospitals. To really make a difference, it needs to be a bilateral and collaborative effort. As a first step, we will be collaborating with Washinzaka Hospital, a hospital CHCP group recently invested in, to further align and partner with medical institutions on improving patients’ path toward hospital discharge and transition to regional care.

**Tatsunari：** As I mentioned earlier, facilities and services that cater to patients following hospital discharges have grown and diversified. N. Field’s mission is to promote integrated regional care systems for patients nationwide, and we hope to make significant progress in supporting patients in their reintegration into their communities.

**Eri:** Thank you for your time today. I am excited about what the future holds for N.Field in bettering the lives of its patients, and the unique model that can potentially be built with the CHCP group,

## Okamoto Hospital and Kyowa Pharmaceutical: treatment evolution and discharge support

Kenji Ueno (Okamoto Hospital Administrative Manager)

**Kenji:** Traditionally, patients diagnosed with a CNS-related condition required months to years of hospitalization for treatment in Japan. Today, hospitalization periods have been significantly reduced with diversified treatment options. The history behind this dates back to the Meiji Era and serves as the basis of the current “Act on Mental Health and Welfare for the Mentally Disabled”, in which patients were required to be cared for at home in confinement. A directive implemented in the 1960s allowed for psychiatric hospitals to be operated with fewer doctors and nurses, leading to a surge in the number of psychiatric hospitals and prolonged hospitalizations. From the 2000s, the government began to push for the integrated regional care system as a substitute for prolonged hospitalization. Expediting hospital discharge would not have been made possible without the expansion of care facilities and services, along with pharmaceutical advancements. Polypharmacy has also been reduced with new drug development, and the more effective treatments that are available today have been key to building trust between patients and care providers—with patients commonly fearing that they are taking poison when prescribed with medications, effective medications have worked to build patients’ trust in their care providers.

Shinji Yamamoto (Kyowa Pharmaceutical Industry Sales & Marketing, Head of Marketing Services)

**Shinji:** Traditionally, CNS-related drug treatment was largely symptomatic, meaning prescription sleep aids were prescribed for insomnia, and anti-anxiety medication was prescribed for anxiety. While effective in reducing symptoms, this did not address the root cause, often leading to dependency and long-term usage, making social rehabilitation difficult for patients. CNS-related medical treatment has since evolved to enable patients to recover to their original condition by addressing the root cause of their symptoms. Specifically, these medications work to target symptom-causing abnormalities in the nervous system. While not immediately effective in comparison to symptomatic treatments, side effects and dependency, along with constraints that drug treatments can have on daily life, have been reduced drastically. In fact, recent research has suggested that recovery of cognitive functions (i.e. personal financial management, grooming/dressing appropriately, etc.) are more effective towards social rehabilitation than reducing the symptoms themselves, and much of the new drug development taking place is based on this research. Kyowa Pharmaceutical Industry continues to evolve with the mission of supporting patients in the CNS field in their paths towards recovery.

## CHCP Group: building the collaboration platform for integrated CNS care

Shinsuke Muto (CHCP Group Chairman)

Unison’s CHCP Group aims to improve the quality of regional healthcare services by promoting collaborations across the various healthcare service providers it aggregates. In June, CHCP’s investment in Hakuaikai marked its first investment in a dental clinic. This was followed by its investment in Washinzaka Hospital in July 2022, marking CHCP’s first investment in a psychiatric hospital operator with another similar investment expected to follow by year-end. Plans of extending dental care services for patients with CNS-related health conditions as part of a collaboration between the two portfolio companies are already underway. With N.Field already providing nationwide at-home nursing services for patients with mental health conditions and neurological disorders, the possibilities of further collaboration are boundless.\
\
The success of these collaborations largely relies on being able to recruit top talent and personnel. Despite the implementation of integrated regional care systems, in addition to society’s general lack of preparation for reintegration of patients into their communities after being discharged as pointed out earlier by Tatsunari and Hajime from N.Field, the lack of talent available due to the demands put on medical professionals in this field presents another challenge in helping patients move toward being discharged. As CNS-related health conditions tend to recur, patient rehospitalization often takes place. High medical professional turnover presents an additional obstacle for patients seeking a smooth transition across various healthcare services.\
\
Hiring and retention of care providers in the CNS field is arguably the most difficult even within the broader healthcare industry. Medical professionals often require specific knowledge and expertise in the field which, combined with the psychological burdens incurred by care providers, pose significant recruitment and retention challenges. As an example, Mr. Ueno, introduced earlier, is extremely cautious about maintaining a good work atmosphere within Okamoto Hospital, and spends much of his time motivating and encouraging the hospital’s medical professionals. Similarly, improving hiring and retention have been a key value-add lever of Unison’s investment in N.Field, with work environment reform efforts made post-investment beginning to take effect.

Healthcare services are labor-intensive by nature, and cordial human relationships are key to successful treatment. We believe that a successful integrated CNS regional care system requires improving the working environment of medical professionals, which in turn leads to patient recovery and satisfaction.
