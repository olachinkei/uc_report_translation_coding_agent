# Unison’s Approach to ESG

As stated in the Guiding Principles which embody the underlying philosophy serving as the core of our work as investors, Unison has strived to create social impact from its investment activities since inception (excerpt from Guiding Principles below).

*While we rigorously pursue profitable opportunities, we aim to make an impact on the business community we are involved in.*

*We seek to create business transactions which have value not only to ourselves but also to society.*

Based on the belief that improvements related to both the working environment and employee motivation within our portfolio companies ultimately drive value over the long-term, we have been undertaking such initiatives since Fund I. In response to growing awareness towards ESG, we became a PRI signatory in 2017 and have reorganized our investment activities and reporting to comply with PRI standards since.

It is our unwavering belief that control investments led by private equity funds provide the optimal governance structure to effectively pursue ESG initiatives. Given its influence on portfolio company management, the responsibility private equity investors bear towards achieving ESG goals outweigh that of public market investors. Particularly for small to mid-cap investment strategies like Unison’s, it is often the case that portfolio companies lack the organizational infrastructure and facilities needed to pursue growth, implying ESG goals are very much aligned with financial objectives. In addition, ESG initiatives largely impact exit valuations as well, especially while considering a public market exit, or a trade sale to public company. It is, however, also true that successful ESG-related precedents within Japanese private equity industry are still limited. Under such circumstances, Unison strives to be at the forefront of the industry with its ESG practice.

In 2020, we established the following two-stage ESG framework intended to best address ESG-related issues specific to small to mid-cap companies, which have been applied across our portfolio companies:

## [Stage 1] Community, Diversity & Empowerment

\- C: Contribution to local regional communities and customer communities which the company serves

\- D: Diversity and inclusion (female representation among management positions, board diversity)

\- E: Improvement of working environment (fairness in evaluation and compensation, empowerment of employees through transparent communication and sharing of information)

## [Stage 2] Carbon, Disposables & Energy

\- C: Reduction of carbon emissions

\- D: Reduction of plastic and other environmentally harmful materials

\- E: Improvement in efficiency of water and energy consumption; active utilization of renewable energy

Specific initiatives to be implemented are tailored in accordance with each portfolio company’s mission statement, investment thesis and capacity, among other factors. Furthermore, we focus on initiatives in which achievements and progress can be quantified, enabling for specific goals and targets to be implemented and benchmarked against industry best practices. Ultimately, we seek to measure the quantitative impact achieved throughout our investment term. Lastly, these goals are incorporated within each portfolio company’s mid-term business plan and management evaluation process, in order to ensure alignment between Unison and each portfolio company’s management.

We continue to expand our ESG-related ecosystem and accelerate collaborative initiatives involving external experts in order to remain attentive to global ESG trends, while striving to build a successful track-record of achieving ESG goals within our portfolio companies.

**Junko**: We feel the same. As part of integrating ESG factors across our investments, we came up with a framework comprised of two stages. **Kim**: This framework effectively addresses some of the most material ESG issues both in Japan and Asia, most notably the lack of organizational and institutional diversity, especially regarding gender equality. **Junko**: It’s reassuring to hear that from you. In our partnership with RIMM Sustainability, we seek to establish a way to measure the ESG impact achieved across our portfolio companies utilizing third-party assessment. Being that you also serve as a Scientific Advisory Board member of RIMM, what are your thoughts regarding ESG-related third-party assessment?

**Kim**: Properly functioning ESG disclosure and sustainability reporting, as well as access to consistent and granular corporate ESG data is vital for Sustainable Finance and ESG investment. Currently, many existing ESG ratings face serious challenges in terms of reliability, coverage and methodology. Given these shortcomings, financial supervisors globally are moving towards a more active regulation of ESG data and rating providers. Even for publicly listed companies, the scarcity of quality ESG data has presented challenges to accurately assess ESG performance. The challenge is more relevant to small to mid-cap companies, as ESG data is not systematically collected and these enterprises lack the internal resources to conduct sustainability reporting. That is where third-party assessment providers can play a valuable role, if they can offer a way of streamlining not only the reporting process, but also by making ESG-related expertise more accessible for small to mid-cap companies. That way, the risk of greenwashing will be reduced while also opening credit and investment markets to ESG-aligned small to mid-cap companies.

**Junko**: We’ve discussed this before as well, but there seems to be quite a bit of confusion with the jargon related to Sustainable Finance and ESG – the most notable being “ESG Investing” and “Impact Investing”. How do you define the difference between the two?

**Kim**: Let’s clarify the jargon by first introducing even more jargon – *“ex-ante”* and *“ex-post”*. “*Ex-ante”* means before a project or activity is being carried out; “*ex-post”* means everything after the initial project or activity planning and assessment stages. ESG Investing is primarily a sustainability-related investment strategy that focuses on *“ex-ante”* evaluations, meaning it will consider ESG factors, such as GHG emissions, gender equality or corruption-related controversies, to assess risks and opportunities based on available past performance data. It will, however, not wait for concrete results nor actively forego market-level returns in return for verifiable sustainability impacts. Alpha and beta are still the main KPIs for ESG investment strategies. In contrast, Impact Investing is an *“ex-post”* investment strategy that focuses on the measurement of tangible impacts and often requiring more intense monitoring of investment activities as well as more active engagement of investors. Since Impact Investing is focused on generating verifiable *“ex-post”* impact, it is generally more resource-intensive and is often done as concessional finance below-market interest rates. In terms of global AUM volumes, ESG Investing far outpaces Impact Investing, primarily given the far more involved approach that the latter represents.

**Junko**: This helps clarify the confusion. We are excited to work with you to strengthen our ESG-related value creation capabilities. Let us conclude by talking about some areas in which we can further collaborate.

**Kim**: I too, am excited about the opportunity to work with Unison. Proper integration of ESG factors will not happen overnight and addressing the data-related challenges specific to private equity will require persistence and unique expertise. **Junko:** It sounds like there are a lot more areas in which we can collaborate, and I am very excited about making these ideas take shape. Thank you again for your time today.

We were first introduced to RIMM Sustainability Ltd in the course of searching for a way to objectively quantify the financial impact of the ESG-related initiatives undertaken as part of our investment activities.

At the time, RIMM was a newly founded startup based in Singapore. Being that Unison had a predating working relationship with one of RIMM’s founders, we were able to learn about their vision and their services which were still in preliminary stages of development. RIMM's vision is “to make sustainability simple, accessible, authentic, fun and rewarding for companies of all sizes”. Specifically, RIMM is engaged in the development of a software platform services intended to better visualize and quantify corporate sustainability in accordance with GRI, SASB and other international standards.

The RIMM platform applies a proprietary algorithm incorporating both individual company disclosures as well as RIMM's sustainability database covering public companies globally to yield a company's "RIMM score", intended for assessing corporate sustainability. Its customer interface is designed for intuitive operations, and issues a sustainability report intended for identifying the company's status quo and points for improvement related to sustainability, with an emphasis on detailed impact.

We invested in RIMM in June 2020 and have begun collaborating on the development of a platform applicable to private equity funds targeting small to mid-cap companies. The project has made steady progress, and we plan to be able to implement RIMM’s services across our Fund IV and Fund V portfolio companies towards the end of 2021. This project taking shape will enable us to better visualize corporate sustainability across our portfolio, and offer concrete, detailed advice towards portfolio company management for improving corporate sustainability.

# CHCP Group: redesigning healthcare through regional collaboration

The COVID-19 pandemic was a perfect storm to shed a light on the working environment of medical practitioners and the structural issues deeply rooted in Japan’s healthcare system. While these issues have been amplified by the pandemic, they have always been prevalent given Japan’s aging population.

Japan’s healthcare system is well suited to its culture and is generally considered successful in comparison to other nations. As beneficiaries of this system, we have a strong desire to address these challenges, ultimately leaving an even better healthcare system for generations to follow.

Healthcare service providers face a conundrum in which the demand for higher quality and more efficient services keep increasing, while challenges associated with labor shortage and the lack of successors are also increasingly relevant. The COVID-19 pandemic has magnified these challenges, raising the urgency for revisiting the healthcare system as a whole in Japan.

CHCP Group aggregates various healthcare service providers such as hospitals, community pharmacies, home-nursing services and etc., promoting collaborations across various healthcare service providers to address the abovementioned issues through optimizing staffing, and in some cases, even identifying potential successors. The objective is to build a framework that encompasses patients of the local community by providing a comprehensive care package comprised of acute-care, post-acute care/rehabilitation, and home nursing services.

We are confident that these efforts will not only improve the quality of services offered by healthcare service providers, but also promote greater satisfaction among patients, medical practitioners, and local communities as a whole.

The COVID-19 pandemic has raised attention towards revisiting healthcare systems globally. Hence, the unique solution CHCP Group provides to local communities through promoting collaborations across various different types of healthcare service providers is expected to be increasingly relevant going forward.

Adopting ICT systems and technological innovation is considered key to driving the aforementioned solutions, and this is a key focus area CHCP Group is actively involved in. The COVID-19 pandemic has accelerated such trends, in which certain countries have effectively contained the pandemic through utilizing healthcare-related data, and new service providers have surfaced on the back of digitalization. CHCP Group seeks to optimize healthcare solutions provided within the local communities it serves by seamlessly integrating various healthcare services and medical resources catering to individual patients. In doing so, CHCP Group hopes to actively pursue collaborative initiatives involving research institutions, startups with disruptive technologies, local municipalities, as well as overseas healthcare players to accelerate this process through innovation.\
